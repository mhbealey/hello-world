# Aesthetic Direction — Deep Space Transit Domain

## Reference heritage

- Cassini-class probes: metallic gold/silver bus, large dish antenna, visible science instrument cluster
- NTP/nuclear propulsion concept renders: cylindrical core, large radiator panels at rear, minimal solar arrays
- Deep Space 1 / Dawn: large solar arrays, compact bus, minimal structural complexity
- Mars Science Laboratory cruise stage: disc-shaped, large backshell, visible chord connections

## Material palette

| Surface | Material call | Notes |
|---------|--------------|-------|
| Primary bus structure | White/silver, high specular | Long-duration thermal management surface |
| Radiator panels | White, large, visually dominant | Deep-space thermal rejection requires large surface area |
| RTG housings (if applicable) | Dark grey, cylindrical | Must be distinct — safety visibility requirement |
| Solar array surfaces | Deep blue (if concept uses solar) | Only if within solar constant range |
| Propulsion elements | Metallic, slightly weathered | Plume-facing surfaces show heat staining at Tier 3 |
| Science instruments | Instrument-specific per study | Gold-foil or protective covers in transit configuration |

## Radiator treatment

Deep-space concepts are often radiator-dominated. The radiator panel area should be sized to match the thermal rejection budget in the study — a credibility check. Large, prominent radiator arrays are correct; do not minimize them for aesthetics.

## Weathering convention

Tier 2: none required — transit vehicle in cruise, pristine.
Tier 3: cosmic ray darkening on white surfaces (subtle grey shift), solar panel edge darkening from long-term UV, thruster nozzle heat staining.

## Scale indication

Deep-space transit concepts may need a human figure or reference object for scale. If the concept includes a crew compartment, indicate scale with a 1.8 m reference figure. If the concept is robotic only, indicate scale with a standard 1 m cube in one render.
