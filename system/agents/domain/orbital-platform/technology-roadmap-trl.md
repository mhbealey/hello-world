## Domain overlay: orbital-platform

**Program schedule (IOC 2035 baseline):**
```
2026–2028  Design: Modular architecture definition; truss interface standard; docking standard
2028       GATE A: Interface standards locked (commercial partners commit to standard)
2028–2031  Phase 1 construction: Anchor module + power + 4 docking ports
2031       GATE B: First crew aboard; Phase 1 IOC
2031–2035  Phase 2: Truss expansion, manufacturing modules, propellant depot
2035       IOC: Full spaceport capability (12 crew, 8 docking ports, propellant depot online)
2035–2040  Phase 3: Industrial scale (20+ visiting vehicles/month, in-space manufacturing)
2040       Full commercial operations
```

**Critical technology gaps (prioritized):**
1. **Multi-vehicle traffic management (TRL 3–4):** No flight system manages 20+ concurrent approach corridors. Air traffic control software adapted for orbital mechanics; demonstration required in simulation by 2028, flight demo by 2032.
2. **On-orbit propellant transfer at scale (TRL 4–5):** SpaceX demonstrated in 2025 for Starship-to-Starship; depot receiving standard interface not defined. Lock interface standard by 2028 gate.
3. **Large-structure autonomous assembly (TRL 5–6):** Canadarm2 assembles modules with crew oversight; autonomous assembly of kilometer-scale structures without crew in the loop is TRL 5. Target TRL 7 by 2031.
4. **Commercial crew training pipeline (TRL/process 4–5):** No standard exists for training non-astronaut commercial crew for orbital industrial work. 30-day on-orbit orientation program must be developed and validated by 2033.
5. **In-space manufacturing at commercial scale (TRL 3–5, domain-dependent):** Pharmaceuticals (TRL 6 in microgravity), fiber optics (TRL 5–6), exotic alloys (TRL 3–4). Manufacturing module designs depend on which product lines are commercially viable.

**Key difference from lunar pathfinder:** Most enabling technologies (docking, attitude control, life support, communications) are at TRL 8–9 from ISS heritage. The spaceport technology challenge is integration and scale, not breakthrough capability. Development risk is dominated by commercial standardization (who wins the docking standard) and economics (Starship cost realization), not physics.

**Demonstrations needed:**
- 2027: Full-fidelity traffic management simulation with 20 virtual vehicles
- 2028: Anchor module design review; interface standard ratified
- 2030: Propellant transfer demonstration (Starship tanker to depot prototype)
- 2031: First crew aboard Phase 1; all Phase 1 systems operational
- 2033: Manufacturing module demonstration (select highest-TRL product line)

**Program structure:**
- Phase A (now–2028): Architecture, standards, commercial partner agreements
- Phase B (2028–2031): Phase 1 construction and crewing
- Phase C/D (2031–2035): Phase 2 expansion to IOC
