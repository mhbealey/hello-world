## Domain overlay: orbital-platform

**Crew composition at IOC (~2035):** 8–12 resident crew on rotating 6-month tours. Mixed population: specialist crew (operations, engineering, medical) + commercial operators (manufacturing specialists, researchers). Not all crew are career astronauts — this is an architectural requirement on the operations interface.

**Supervisor ratios for autonomous systems:**
| System type | Human oversight | Autonomy level |
|-------------|----------------|----------------|
| Docking (unmanned vehicles) | 1 operator monitoring N vehicles | 1 operator can monitor 8–12 simultaneous approaches |
| Robotic arm operations | 1 operator per arm, active monitoring | 3–4 simultaneous with current autonomy |
| Manufacturing module | 1 specialist per module bank | Scales with automation TRL |
| Traffic management | 1 controller per 24-hr shift | System-assisted; air traffic model |

**Long-duration microgravity factors:**
- Bone density loss: −1% per month without countermeasures; crew exercise 2.5 hours/day is mandatory and non-negotiable
- Cardiovascular deconditioning: 6-month tours are within the ISS-proven safety envelope; longer tours require additional countermeasures
- Isolation: LEO is not isolated (constant Earth contact); psychological profile differs fundamentally from lunar or deep-space crew — communications constraints are not a primary stressor
- Vision impairment (SANS): Spaceflight Associated Neuro-ocular Syndrome; ~30% of long-duration crew affected; monitoring protocol required

**Commercial occupant considerations:**
- Non-astronaut commercial crew (manufacturing staff, researchers) will be normal by 2035 given Axiom and similar
- Training baseline: commercial crew should reach operational competency in ≤30 days on-orbit orientation (contrast with 18-month astronaut training)
- Interface requirement: all operational systems must have a "novice mode" accessible to trained-but-non-expert operators within 2 years of study IOC target
- Safety: no more complex than operating heavy industrial equipment; commercial standards apply

**Shift structure and cognitive load:**
- Three 8-hour shifts (24/7 coverage); 2 crew minimum per shift for buddy system (ISS standard)
- Traffic management peak load: docking windows cluster around dawn/dusk orbital passes; shift timing should account for this
- Monotony countermeasure: varied task assignment across manufacturing, maintenance, monitoring, research; pure monitoring shifts > 4 hours degrade vigilance

**Heritage:**
- ISS crew productivity: ~6 hours effective work/person/day; ISS-heritage crew planning applies
- Axiom/private astronaut missions: commercial crew in ISS environment; operations interface lessons
- Offshore oil platform operations: commercial crew in hazardous environment, shift work, remote deployment — conceptual analog for industrial spaceport crew model
- Antarctic stations: winter-over psychology (less relevant — LEO crew have daily Earth contact)
