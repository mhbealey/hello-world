## Domain overlay: orbital-platform (orbital station and commercial space heritage)

**Primary heritage threads for this study:**

**1. ISS program (1998–present) — primary analog**
- Mass: 420 t; length: 109 m; 16 pressurized modules
- Power: 215 kW (solar); 8 CMGs for attitude control
- Assembly: 42 US Space Shuttle flights + 36 Russian Soyuz/Progress + 3 European/Japanese vehicles
- Key lessons: (a) on-orbit assembly is feasible but expensive and time-consuming; (b) modular truss architecture is proven; (c) international partnerships complicate operations significantly; (d) EVA hours required for assembly far exceeded predictions
- Sources: NASA SP-2015-624 (ISS Benefits for Humanity); NASA OIG program cost reports

**2. Mir (1986–2001) — sustainment heritage**
- 15-year operational life through aggressive in-flight repair
- Multiple serious failures survived: fire (1997), collision (1997), ADCS anomalies
- Key lesson: design for repair, not replacement; crew competency in technical repair is a design requirement
- Costs: Soviet-era costs are not directly comparable; Western estimates ~$1B/year operations

**3. Skylab (1973–1974) — structural heritage**
- First large-volume US orbital station; 77 t, 12 kW solar
- Loss of main solar array on launch; repaired on first crewed mission — demonstrates in-space repair capability
- Key lesson: launch damage recovery via EVA; EVA capability must be planned, not assumed

**4. Commercial stations (planned, 2025–2035)**
- Axiom Station: commercial modules attached to ISS; first commercial residents 2025; heritage for commercial crew integration
- Vast Station (Haven-1): SpaceX Dragon-launched commercial station; 2025 target
- Starlab (Voyager/Nanoracks): inflatable commercial station with inflatable module (Bigelow heritage)
- Key insight: none of these reach 100 t, let alone 1,500 t; spaceport scale requires a step change from current commercial plans

**5. Propellant depot concepts (not yet flown)**
- NASA MXER tether and propellant transfer concepts: studied but not demonstrated
- SpaceX Starship tanker: in-space propellant transfer demonstrated in 2025 (Starship flight 7/8 tests)
- ULA ACES upper stage: designed for propellant storage; TRL 6 for long-duration cryogenic management
- Key gap: on-orbit propellant transfer at the scale required by a full spaceport (hundreds of tonnes/month) has no flight heritage

**6. Autonomous docking heritage (most directly applicable)**
- SpaceX Dragon: fully autonomous docking to ISS since 2020; RTLT-independent; TRL 9
- Northrop Grumman MEV-1/2: autonomous rendezvous with non-cooperative satellites; TRL 9
- Russian Progress: automated docking (Kurs system) since 1985; thousands of successful dockings
- Key insight: autonomous docking to a cooperative port is solved; traffic management at 20+ vehicles/month is not

**Research priority order:** ISS assembly lessons → Mir sustainment protocols → Commercial station commercial crew integration → Propellant transfer heritage → Traffic management architecture analogs (air traffic control).
