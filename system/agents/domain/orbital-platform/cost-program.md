## Domain overlay: orbital-platform

**Program type:** Commercial development with government anchor customers. Revenue model applies: this is a commercial spaceport, not a government capability program. Cost-reduction to LEO is both the mechanism and the business case.

**Scale anchors (concept phase, ±50% margins apply):**
| Item | Cost estimate | Basis |
|------|--------------|-------|
| Falcon 9 per launch (current) | ~$67M | SpaceX published pricing |
| Starship per launch (target) | $10–50M | SpaceX aspirational; assume $50M for planning |
| ISS total program cost | ~$150B (1998–2030) | NASA OIG, multiple estimates |
| ISS per kg on orbit | ~$350K/kg | $150B / ~420 t |
| Starship per kg to LEO | $100–500/kg | SpaceX target; assume $500/kg for planning |
| Phase 1 build-out (100 t) | $10–20B | $500/kg × 100 t = $50M launch + hardware + integration |
| Full build-out (1,500 t) | $50–150B | Order-of-magnitude from ISS scaling, better launch economics |

**The launch cost lever:** At $67M/Falcon 9 (22 t to LEO = $3,000/kg), the 1,500 t spaceport would cost $4.5B in launch costs alone, excluding hardware. At $100/kg Starship target, the same mass costs $150M in launch — 30× cost reduction. The entire commercial case depends on Starship economics materializing.

**Revenue model (concept phase):**
- Propellant depot services: resale of propellant at orbit-delivered price; margin on transportation cost reduction for customers
- Module leasing: commercial operators lease habitation/manufacturing modules at station
- Transit hub fees: visiting vehicles pay docking and logistics fees (airport-model)
- In-space manufacturing: premium products that require microgravity (pharmaceuticals, fiber optics, exotic alloys)

**Cost timeline:**
- 2026–2030 (formulation + phase 1 design): ~$2–5B R&D + hardware
- 2030–2035 (Phase 1 construction): $10–20B (launch dominant)
- 2035–2040 (Phase 2 expansion + ops): $15–30B (mix of launch + ops revenue)
- 2040+ (full commercial ops): revenue-positive scenario requires ≥$2B/year revenue at full build-out

**Critical sensitivities:**
1. Starship cost realization: if Starship stays at $100M/launch, the commercial case collapses
2. Crew rotation cadence: 8-person crew at 6-month tours = 16 crew seats/year; at $50M/seat (Crew Dragon heritage) that's $800M/year just for crew transport
3. Revenue timeline: at IOC, revenue from manufacturing/depot lags investment by 3–5 years — bridge financing required
4. Regulatory framework: no in-space manufacturing regulatory regime exists; jurisdictional ambiguity is a business risk, not just legal

**Comparison to reference programs:** ISS at $150B is the ceiling of what government can fund. This platform targets $50–150B over 20 years — same order, with commercial revenue offsetting government contributions after IOC.
