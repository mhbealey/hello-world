Abstract

(Draft. Update as study matures.)

This concept study examines a humanoid-forward architecture for space exploration, with the lunar far side permanent base as the proving ground. The thesis is that humanoid-forward architecture is the unlock for three things current crewed-only and crewed-plus-rovers architectures cannot deliver: economically sustainable presence across the inner and middle solar system, the in-space industrial base that enables future deep space and eventual interstellar capability, and persistent scientific operations at destinations beyond credible crewed reach.

The study is organized around four questions: what does an optimal space humanoid look like; does human-in-the-loop add value, and where; what is the workflow; and how do we build it and where do we go. The lunar far side base is treated as the testbed where the human-humanoid teaming model is proven before extension to cislunar, Mars orbit, and beyond.

Soviet and Russian engineering heritage threads through the analysis: Lunokhod for teleoperation with delay, Mir and Salyut for long-duration human factors, Mars-500 for isolation studies, and Soviet lunar base concepts for the testbed.
