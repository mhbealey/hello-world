# Heritage Baseline: Orbital Industrial Spaceport

## Purpose

Establish the heritage anchor for the orbital spaceport study. Every number proposed in subsequent cycles must be traceable to one of these precedents or justified as a deviation.

---

## Primary Precedent: ISS (1998–present)

The ISS is the only kilometer-class orbital assembly in history and the baseline for every structural, operational, and cost estimate in this study.

**Key parameters:**
- Total mass: 420 t (on-orbit)
- Pressurized volume: 916 m³
- Length: 109 m (S-truss to P-truss)
- Power: 215 kW (solar arrays)
- Crew: 6–7 resident (rotating 6-month tours)
- Assembly: 42 Space Shuttle flights, 1998–2011
- Total program cost: ~$150B (1998–2030 estimate, NASA OIG)
- Cost per kg on orbit: ~$357K/kg

**Lessons directly applicable to this study:**
1. Modular truss-and-node assembly is flight-proven at this scale. The S1/P1 truss segments (13.7 m each, 14 t total) are the heritage for our Phase 1 truss design.
2. Assembly requires far more EVA hours than predicted: ISS required >1,500 EVA hours for assembly. Budget accordingly.
3. International partnerships created operational complexity (common berthing mechanisms, two separate segments with different systems). Commercial equivalent: proprietary vs. open docking standards.
4. Crew productivity ~6 effective hours/person/day after systems maintenance overhead — this is the planning baseline for all crew-dependent operations.

**Where this study diverges:** Scale (5× ISS at full build-out), commercial ownership, industrial operations tempo (20+ vehicles/month vs. ISS ~12/year), propellant depot function.

## Secondary Precedent: Mir (1986–2001)

Fifteen-year operational life despite multiple serious failures. The sustainment philosophy is the lesson, not the technology.

- Design-for-repair enabled survival of: chemical fire (1997), Progress collision (1997), multiple ADCS anomalies
- Crew as repair technicians: cosmonauts performed repairs that were not in original design specs
- Application to this study: all replaceable components must be ORU-designed; crew training must include repair scenarios, not just nominal operations

## Tertiary Precedent: Commercial Station Programs (2025–2035)

None of the current commercial programs reach spaceport scale, but they establish commercial operations heritage:

| Program | Mass | Crew | Launch vehicle |
|---------|------|------|---------------|
| Axiom Station | ~60 t | 8 | Falcon 9 (Dragon) |
| Vast Haven-1 | ~10 t | 4 | Falcon 9 (Dragon) |
| Starlab (Nanoracks) | ~20 t inflatable | 4 | Falcon 9 |
| Tiangong-3 | 100 t | 3 | Long March |

**Key insight:** No current commercial program approaches 500 t. The spaceport requires a step change from current commercial plans, enabled by Starship-class launch economics (§A1).

## Autonomous Docking Heritage

| System | Status | Approach type |
|--------|--------|--------------|
| SpaceX Dragon | TRL 9, operational | Fully autonomous to cooperative port |
| Northrop MEV-1/2 | TRL 9, operational | Autonomous to non-cooperative satellite |
| Russian Progress (Kurs) | TRL 9, 40-year record | Automated to cooperative port |
| Boeing Starliner | TRL 7–8 | Autonomous with ground monitoring |

**Finding:** Docking to a single cooperative port is solved. Traffic management of 20+ simultaneous vehicles in approach corridors has no flight heritage — this is the critical gap.

---

## Heritage Gaps (No Flight Precedent)

1. **Propellant transfer at scale:** SpaceX demonstrated Starship-to-Starship in 2025, but depot operations at 100+ t/month are unflown.
2. **Multi-vehicle orbital traffic management:** Air traffic control software is the conceptual model; space application is TRL 3–4.
3. **Commercial non-astronaut industrial crew operations:** Axiom demonstrates private astronauts; industrial workers operating manufacturing equipment in microgravity is undemonstrated.
4. **In-space manufacturing at commercial scale:** Microgravity manufacturing of pharmaceuticals, fiber optics, and exotic alloys has been demonstrated at research scale; commercial throughput is TRL 3–5 depending on product.
