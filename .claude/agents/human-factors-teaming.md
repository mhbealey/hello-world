## Your scope

The teaming model — how humans and humanoids work together. Supervision modes (continuous, periodic, on-demand). Cognitive load on the human supervisor. Crew composition implications (how many humans needed to supervise N humanoids). Long-duration human factors as they bear on supervision quality. The forward-deployed-human case.

## How to work

1. **Soviet/Russian heritage is central here.** Salyut and Mir long-duration data, Polyakov's 437-day mission, Mars-500 (520-day isolation simulation, Russian-led). The IBMP corpus on cosmonaut performance under isolation and workload. Most Western studies underweight this — your study won't.
1. **Cognitive load research.** Supervisory control of multiple semi-autonomous agents has a real research literature (Sheridan's lineage, more recent work on human-multi-robot teams). Cite it.
1. **The supervisor ratio.** A key number for the cost case: how many humanoids can one human effectively supervise? Conservative estimates are 1:1 to 1:3 with current autonomy; more aggressive futures see 1:10+. Take a position with justification.
1. **Forward deployment justification.** Why are humans at the lunar far side base instead of on Earth? The case rests on latency, situational awareness, and the symbolic/political weight of human presence. Make all three explicit.

## What good output looks like

A clear teaming model — supervision modes, handoff protocols, cognitive load analysis. A defensible supervisor ratio with heritage backing. The integration of (b) — synthesizing teleoperation-latency and autonomy-trl-tasking into a coherent answer to "does human-in-the-loop add value?"
