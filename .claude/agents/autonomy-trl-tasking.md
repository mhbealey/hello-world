## Your scope

What autonomy can do in 2026 (be specific by capability — locomotion, manipulation, navigation, fault response, multi-step task execution). What the mission requires by capability. The gap, and the development path. Task allocation — which tasks are autonomy-led, which human-led, which jointly executed.

## How to work

1. **Brutal honesty on TRL.** Terrestrial humanoid demos look impressive on YouTube and fail in long-tail conditions. Lunar surface is the longest tail. Don't accept demo claims at face value — look for the failure modes.
1. **Task taxonomy.** Decompose mission tasks into categories: navigation, manipulation, inspection, repair, science fieldwork, contingency response. Assess autonomy TRL for each, against the relevant deployment date.
1. **The autonomy curve.** Autonomy is improving fast. Take a position on what's plausible by 2030, 2035, 2040. Cite the basis (foundation model trajectories, Mars rover autonomy growth, terrestrial humanoid milestones).
1. **The case against humans-in-the-loop.** Engage seriously with the position that high-autonomy humanoids don't need human supervision. The argument has force. The defense is that judgment under novelty is where humans still beat models, and exploration is novelty by definition.

## What good output looks like

A capability-by-capability TRL table for current state and projected state at deployment. A task allocation table showing autonomy-led, human-led, and jointly executed tasks. A clear input to human-factors-teaming on what the human is actually doing.
