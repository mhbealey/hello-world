# ADR-001: Adopt v1.0 cycle-based operating cadence

## Status

Accepted.

## Context

The v0.1 system ran eight stages of the lunar pathfinder study. Retros documented five structural problems:
1. Stages are bespoke — no repeating shape, every planning conversation reinvents scaffolding
2. Stage scope drifts mid-stage (stage 6 is the documented example)
3. Review timing is inconsistent — content lived in draft 4–6 weeks before any reviewer touched it
4. No systematic between-stages reflection driving future planning
5. Handbacks grew to 152KB — too heavy for planning-conversation use

## Decision

Replace "stages" with a fixed four-phase cycle structure: Plan → Execute → Review → Reflect/Hand-off.

Key changes from v0.1:
- Every cycle has the same shape regardless of scope
- Review is in-cycle (not batched to a later stage)
- Operator has exactly four touchpoints per cycle (not "every meaningful decision")
- Handback is bounded at 3,000 words (pointers to detail, not detail inline)
- Helper agents may be spawned when dispatches go long (§11.9 of the cadence)

## Consequences

**Positive:**
- Planning conversations fill a template rather than inventing structure
- Content without review cannot close a cycle — the failure mode from v0.1 is structurally prevented
- Retro observations drive scaffold inputs by design (required field in template)
- Operator becomes an oversight role rather than an in-loop role

**Negative:**
- Cycles take longer than v0.1 stages because review is in-cycle
- More structure to maintain (cycle scaffold, dispatch log, handback gate)

The in-cycle review cost is the right trade: v0.1's batch-review pattern produced cleaner per-stage metrics but worse content quality at the question level.
